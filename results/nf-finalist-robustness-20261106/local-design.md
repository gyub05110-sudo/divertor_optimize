# OMP interpolation attribution design

Primary pair: existing 640-donor cubic and quintic exports of the selected finite-grid OMP R020 wall at χ = 0.3 m²/s. Use the identical audit at χ = 0.6 and on both matched baselines. Freeze the archived walls, operating points, maps, interpolation policy and its 1% local limit. Record current and historical candidate vector IDs and maximum wall-coordinate differences separately.

1. Locate the unsigned maximum difference of the policy's two piecewise-linear saved q profiles on physical arclength, without alignment. Report signed quintic minus cubic change there, at the corresponding native ring, and the global peak locations and A₂.
2. Reconstruct collapsed donor weights from complete per-region power rows and compare their sums with prescribed power; independently check geometric hit centers and convert physical widths using 2πR. Use production wrapped Gaussian density and interval mass. Replay every native cell average. Verify pointwise reconstruction at the policy maximum, allowing separately measured saved-sample interpolation residual.
3. At the fixed policy location and its enclosing native ring, evaluate all eight combinations of cubic/quintic center, physical width and weight. Center includes hit radius used to convert physical width to area. Apportion changes by averaging increments over all six orders (Shapley). Keep zero-based donor identities and signs; rank by absolute donor change. Check exact telescoping closure. Report the source-abscissa difference for paired donor indices.
4. Examine the dominant contributor, neighboring donor IDs and all first-hit segment switches. Distinguish adjacent parent-segment crossings from jumps in physical arclength, and compare target width, target gradient and path length. Outputs are model sensitivities conditional on the fixed comparison, not evidence that either interpolation is exact.
