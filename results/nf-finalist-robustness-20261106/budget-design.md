# Retrospective fine-verification budget comparison

This analysis plan was written before computing the new comparator. The six panel datasets, their exhaustive cubic1280 outcomes and the original 200-second policy comparison had already been examined and published. This comparison is exploratory. No geometry, operating input, score or eligibility threshold will be fitted or changed.

- Use all six 12-candidate panels in the frozen study (OMP and IMP, seeds 5161, 7753 and 9923). Preserve candidate order and its three consecutive groups of four.
- Give each policy the fine-verified DIII-D baseline and exactly one new cubic1280 verification per group. Both evaluate all 12 candidates with the original cubic320 screen and see its eligibility decisions. The score policy verifies the eligible candidate with the smallest coarse peak, with ties going to the earliest candidate. The comparator chooses uniformly among those same eligible candidates independently in each group and ignores the numerical coarse scores. A fine-ineligible choice consumes its call and cannot become the incumbent. A group with no eligible choice would make this fixed-count comparison undefined and must be reported.
- For each panel, measure the smallest eligible verified fine peak, allowing retention of the baseline. The finite oracle is the minimum of the baseline and all 12 fine-eligible peaks, including candidates outside the coarse-eligible set. Relative regret (%) is 100 times (verified minimum / finite oracle − 1).
- The primary endpoint is the equally weighted mean across the six panels of expected random-policy regret minus score-policy regret, in percentage points. Enumerate the Cartesian product of eligible choices without simulation. Report the random outcome distribution, its probability of matching or beating the score result including ties, and cost per panel. Check the full distribution against the product of independent within-group survival counts.
- Charge both policies both path setups, baseline evaluation, measured generation including rejected attempts, all 12 coarse calls, and the durations of the three selected fine calls using the original accounting. The fixed-count comparison does not impose a 200-second deadline. Compare separately with the original six-panel 200-second measured-cost replay and the additional two-process OMP deadline run. Interpret all results conditional on the fixed panels and single equilibrium.
